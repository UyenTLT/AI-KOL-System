Correction worth recording: the metric used all week to measure humour was invalid. Checked against two labelled datasets, it fires on 0.3% of lines that actually made a person laugh, and slightly more often on deliberately flat answers than funny ones. Whether she is funnier is therefore a human judgement, not a measured one. The gains above use other, validated measures.

3. Current problem — making her answer more intelligently

  • She invents specifics. Asked for her usual café she names "Café Buena Vista in Little Havana, owner Maria." It does not exist. Humour training worsened this: the confident exact detail that lands a joke also makes a fake business sound real.
  • Biography drifts. After training, 1 answer in 6 about where she lives is wrong (she claims to have lived in Taiwan; she lives in Miami).
  • Root cause: retrieval supplies her real life to the model, but nothing checks the answer against it. There is no step that asks "is this fact in her file?"
  • Proposed fix: verify named entities in each reply against life.json, and refuse or regenerate when a place, person or business appears that is not hers. This is code, not prompting — every prompt-based fix attempted this week failed to move behaviour.

4. How RAG works here

  • Store: life.json, ~130 hand-written facts in typed sections — stories, biography, opinions, people, ongoing threads, knowledge.
  • Retrieval: keyword overlap, not embeddings. The choice is deliberate: ranking ~50 short strings does not justify an embedding model inside the response budget.
  • Anchors bypass retrieval. Five core facts are injected every turn. Sampling a fact means that on turns it is not drawn she has no evidence for her own biography — which is how she once denied a trip written in her own file.
  • Per-character alias map. "Taiwan" matches nothing, because every story names Taipei, Tainan or Jiufen. The alias map expands the query so a place is found under all its names.
  • Conditional sections. Biography and factual knowledge load only when the question reaches for them; unused reference costs tokens in and seconds of speech out.
  • Also used in training. Retrieval runs during dataset generation too, so the model is fitted on answers already grounded in her real life.
